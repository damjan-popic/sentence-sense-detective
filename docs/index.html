About Sentence Sense Detective

Sentence Sense Detective is a free grammar-practice tool developed at the Department of Translation Studies, Faculty of Arts, University of Ljubljana. It turns short, highlighted examples into ten-question practice rounds on parts of speech, sentence elements, and clauses. A correct first answer earns a point; after a mistake, the learner gets one unscored retry and a concise explanation.

How the question bank is built

The English pilot began with 106 examples prepared and reviewed by Martin Grad. To expand the bank, we use openly licensed language corpora and automatic linguistic analysis with Stanza and Universal Dependencies to identify candidate words, sentence elements, and clauses. These candidates are converted into the grammatical terminology used in teaching, filtered, tested, and corrected over time. The technical annotation is part of corpus preparation only: it is not the terminology students are expected to learn.

Our aim

Our immediate aim is an open English practice corpus based on approximately 10,000 sentences, from which varied questions can be selected at random for short practice rounds. In the longer term, we want Sentence Sense Detective to become a reusable multilingual learning tool for the languages taught at the Department of Translation Studies and across the Faculty of Arts.

About the authors

Martin Grad — Principal author and grammar lead

Martin Grad teaches English language and grammar at the Department of Translation Studies, Faculty of Arts, University of Ljubljana. He defines the grammatical framework, prepares and reviews the core examples, and is responsible for the pedagogical analyses and explanations.

Damjan Popič — Co-author and project lead

Damjan Popič is a linguist and language-technology researcher at the Department of Translation Studies and the University of Ljubljana Faculty of Computer and Information Science. He is responsible for the corpus workflow, digital development, open infrastructure, and the planned multilingual expansion.

Sources, openness, and privacy

The code and, wherever source licences permit, the derived learning data are openly available through the project’s GitHub repository. Corpus sources and licences are documented on the Sources and licences page. The practice site requires no account and uses no analytics or tracking; progress is stored only in the learner’s browser.

Methodological introduction

From corpus to classroom practice

This chapter explains how Sentence Sense Detective was built: where the example sentences came from, how they were selected and annotated, how the Stanza analyses were remapped into the grammatical categories used in teaching, and how approved analyses became practice questions.

Additional chapters on word classes, sentence elements, subordination, and terminological policy are in preparation.

Learning goals

After working through this chapter, you should be able to:

  • identify the corpora used for the English question bank and explain why provenance matters;
  • describe what the Stanza pipeline adds to each sentence;
  • explain why technical annotation cannot simply be displayed as classroom grammar;
  • describe the role of pedagogical remapping and expert review;
  • explain how an approved grammatical analysis becomes a practice question.

1. The learning design

Sentence Sense Detective is a practice resource, not an annotation viewer. Students see one highlighted target, choose among four answers, receive immediate feedback, and complete short ten-question rounds. The current English profile separates three related but distinct levels of analysis.

Practice modeCentral questionExamples of answers
Parts of SpeechWhat kind of word is highlighted?noun, pronoun, adjective, lexical verb, auxiliary, preposition
Sentence ElementsWhat function does the highlighted unit have?S, P, Operator, DO, IO, SC, OC, A
ClausesWhat kind of clause is highlighted, how is it formed, and what function does it have?nominal, adverbial, relative; finite, infinitival, -ing; S, DO, A, PostM

Form and function

A word class, a phrase type, and a sentence function are different descriptions. In Sarah and her daughters arrived first, individual words have word-class labels, the complete sequence is a coordinated noun phrase, and the whole phrase functions as the Subject.

2. How the question bank was made

The English release follows one documented pipeline. Each stage has a separate purpose, and the output of one stage becomes the input to the next.

1 · CorpusSelect usable sentences

Keep original wording, provenance, and licence information.

2 · AnnotationAnalyse with Stanza

Add tokens, lemmas, word classes, grammatical features, and dependency structure.

3 · RemappingApply the teaching profile

Convert technical evidence into classroom categories and full target spans.

4 · PracticeBuild the question

Add a prompt, plausible alternatives, feedback, and a concise explanation.

3. Where the sentences came from

The current English corpus contains exactly 10,000 unique sentences. The primary source is MASC 3.0.0, the Manually Annotated Sub-Corpus of the Open American National Corpus. When the filtered MASC material did not supply the full target, the remaining sentences were selected from the written Open American National Corpus.

The resulting collection contains 7,781 MASC sentences and 2,219 written-OANC sentences. It covers a range of written and spoken genres while remaining a contemporary American-English profile. A later British-English or other regional profile can be added without changing the practice interface.

Selection and filtering

Sentences were retained only when they were complete enough to be understood independently and suitable for a general university learning resource. The pipeline removed duplicates, fragments, broken sentence boundaries, code-like material, private information, and content that depended too heavily on unavailable context. It also limited the contribution of individual documents so that the question bank would not be dominated by a small number of sources.

Why provenance matters

Every selected sentence keeps its source corpus, document, genre, licence, and attribution. The public Sources and licences page records the corpus-level information, while the fuller provenance remains in the project data.

4. How the sentences were annotated

All 10,000 sentences were processed locally with the same English Stanza pipeline. The pipeline tokenised the text, handled multiword tokens, assigned parts of speech and grammatical features, produced lemmas, and calculated dependency relations. The analysis was stored in both a portable CoNLL-U representation and a JSONL form that preserves exact character offsets.

The dependency analysis follows the Universal Dependencies framework. This gives the project a consistent technical representation of words and their structural relations, but it is not the terminology students are expected to learn. Its purpose is to provide reproducible evidence for the next stage.

One reproducible analysis path

Even where a source corpus already contains linguistic annotation, the project runs the same Stanza pipeline over the selected material. This keeps the workflow repeatable and makes it easier to apply the same architecture to additional languages later.

5. How the Stanza annotations were remapped

The technical analysis and the teaching analysis answer different questions. A dependency representation identifies structural heads and relations; a grammar exercise may require the complete phrase, a clause-level function, a distinction such as Operator versus Predicator, or a construction that cannot be decided from one technical label alone.

The project therefore uses a separate, versioned pedagogical remapping profile. The profile specifies what evidence supports a teaching category, what evidence blocks it, how the complete highlighted target is reconstructed, and whether the result may be published automatically or should be sent for expert review.

Direct correspondence

The source analysis and the classroom category align cleanly, and the target span is unambiguous.

Structural interpretation

Several pieces of evidence must be combined, for example to recognise a copular pattern, an Operator, or a clause functioning inside a larger clause.

Expert review

The analysis depends on context, attachment, meaning, or a theoretical choice. The item is withheld rather than forced into the public bank.

The reviewed reference set

The English profile is anchored in 106 examples prepared and reviewed by Martin Grad. They include 26 direct correspondences, 60 structurally derived cases, and 20 cases that require expert review. The implemented remapper reproduces the labels, target spans, and publication decisions of all 106 reference cases exactly.

Why remapping is not simple renaming

The old woman living in the basement needs help.

A technical analysis identifies a structural centre, but the useful Subject target is the complete noun phrase. Likewise, in Did the children play?, the auxiliary and the lexical verb must be separated into Operator and Predicator. In other cases, such as ambiguous prepositional phrases or reduced clauses, the system must abstain and request review.

6. How an analysis becomes a question

Once a pedagogical annotation is approved, the presentation layer creates the exercise. It does not decide the grammar. It receives a sentence, one or more exact target spans, the answer category, and an explanation, then adds a prompt and three alternatives from the same analytical dimension.

One sentence can support more than one valid question, but an ordinary round never repeats the same sentence. Questions are divided into small files and loaded only when a student starts a round, which keeps the static GitHub Pages site responsive even with a large bank.

From annotation to exercise

She meant no harm.

Prompt: What is the function of the highlighted phrase?

Answer: DO — Direct Object

Feedback: The highlighted noun phrase is directly selected by the verb and functions as its Direct Object.

7. Quality control and correction

The large question bank is not treated as finished merely because it was generated successfully. The reviewed reference set acts as a regression test, lower-confidence constructions remain in a review workbook, and every public question can be reported through GitHub. Corrections are versioned rather than silently replacing earlier analyses.

102,147corpus-derived questions published
17,114candidates retained for review
  1. Preserve the source. Accepted corpus sentences are not rewritten to make the analysis easier.
  2. Separate evidence from presentation. The interface displays an already justified analysis.
  3. Prefer omission to misinformation. Uncertain cases are reviewed rather than published with false confidence.
  4. Keep corrections traceable. Rule changes, reviewed decisions, and data versions remain inspectable in the repository.

8. From English to a multilingual resource

The reusable part of Sentence Sense Detective is the architecture: corpus ingestion, automatic annotation, a versioned remapping profile, exact target spans, review workflow, question delivery, and the practice interface. The grammar itself is not assumed to be identical across languages.

Each additional language will need its own corpus sources, automatic processing pipeline, approved classroom terminology, reviewed examples, grammatical rules, and explanations. This makes it possible to share the platform while respecting the grammatical traditions of the languages taught at the Department of Translation Studies and across the Faculty of Arts.

Transfer principle

The technical workflow can be shared; the pedagogical grammar must be developed for each language.

9. Check your understanding

  1. Why were both MASC and the written OANC used?
  2. What information does Stanza add to a sentence?
  3. Why is a Universal Dependencies analysis not itself a classroom answer?
  4. What are the three possible outcomes of the remapping stage?
  5. Why does the question generator remain separate from the grammatical remapper?
Suggested answers
  1. MASC was the primary source, while written OANC supplied the documented shortfall needed to reach 10,000 suitable unique sentences.
  2. It adds tokenisation, lemmas, word-class and feature information, dependency structure, and exact offsets.
  3. The technical representation is designed for computational consistency; classroom analysis may require different categories, complete target spans, and construction-level interpretation.
  4. A direct correspondence, a structurally derived analysis, or expert review.
  5. The presentation layer should display a justified analysis, not make grammatical decisions through interface code.

10. Chapter summary

  • The English bank is based on 10,000 licensed MASC and written-OANC sentences.
  • Every sentence was processed through the same local Stanza and Universal Dependencies pipeline.
  • A separate pedagogical profile remaps technical evidence into classroom grammar and reconstructs the full target.
  • The 106 reviewed cases anchor the profile, while uncertain constructions remain outside the public bank.
  • The question layer adds prompts, alternatives, feedback, and scoring without deciding the grammar.
  • The same architecture can support additional languages through language-specific profiles.

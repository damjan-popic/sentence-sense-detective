Methodological introduction

From sentence to analysis — and through remapping

Sentence Sense Detective asks students to analyse a highlighted word, phrase, or clause and then compare their decision with a concise explanation. This chapter introduces the grammatical levels used in the tool and the formal remapping process that turns corpus annotation into classroom-ready questions.

Additional chapters on word classes, sentence elements, subordination, and terminological policy are in preparation.

Learning goals

After working through this chapter, you should be able to:

  • distinguish word class, sentence element, and clause analysis;
  • define pedagogical remapping and explain why it is more than renaming labels;
  • distinguish direct correspondences, structurally derived analyses, and cases requiring expert review;
  • explain why a learning target may be a whole phrase, clause, or discontinuous unit;
  • understand how the project expands a question bank without presenting uncertainty as fact.

1. Three levels of grammatical analysis

One expression can receive several compatible descriptions because different questions are being asked. In Sarah and her daughters arrived first, for example, Sarah is a proper noun, daughters is a noun, the complete sequence is a coordinated noun phrase, and the whole phrase functions as the Subject (S).

LevelCentral questionTypical answers
Parts of speechWhat kind of word is highlighted?noun, pronoun, adjective, lexical verb, auxiliary, preposition
Sentence elementsWhat function does the highlighted unit have?S, P, Operator, DO, IO, SC, OC, A
ClausesWhat kind of clause is it, how is it formed, and what function does it have?nominal, adverbial, relative; finite, infinitival, -ing; S, DO, A, PostM

Key principle

Grammatical form and grammatical function are related, but they are not identical. A word-class label cannot by itself determine a sentence-element function, and a clause structure cannot by itself determine the function of that clause in the larger sentence.

2. Formal pedagogical remapping

Remapping is the central methodological contribution of Sentence Sense Detective. It is the explicit, versioned process that connects a computational description of a sentence to the grammatical categories used in teaching. Stanza and Universal Dependencies supply the source analysis; the target layer contains the word classes, sentence elements, clause types, markers, structures, and functions practised by students.

This is not front-end relabelling. A rule may need to inspect several words and relations, recognise a clause pattern, recover the complete phrase or clause to be highlighted, apply an exclusion, resolve rule priority, and decide that the evidence is insufficient for automatic publication.

1 · SourceCorpus sentence

An authentic sentence with preserved wording, licence, and provenance.

2 · AnalysisStanza and UD

Tokens, lemmas, features, heads, relations, and exact character offsets.

3 · RemappingPedagogical profile

Versioned rules reconstruct the teaching analysis and its full target span.

4 · LearningPractice question

An approved annotation becomes a highlighted question with feedback.

Three possible outcomes

Direct correspondence

The source evidence, target extent, and classroom category align without a construction-specific inference.

Structurally derived analysis

The result is licensed only when an explicit grammatical configuration, lexical condition, and span strategy are satisfied.

Expert review

The evidence is incomplete or the construction depends on context or theoretical policy, so the candidate is withheld.

The reviewed contract. The English profile is grounded in 106 cases prepared and reviewed by Martin Grad: 26 direct correspondences, 60 structurally derived analyses, and 20 cases requiring expert review. With the pinned Stanza pipeline, the implemented remapper reproduces all 106 labels, target spans, and publication decisions exactly. No review-only case is automatically published.

The formal profile was applied to 10,000 openly reusable corpus sentences. It generated 274,198 pedagogical candidates, cleared 257,084 for presentation, and routed 17,114 to review. The presentation stage then produced 102,147 corpus-derived questions while keeping the 106 reviewed reference questions visible in the public bank.

Why remapping matters

Without a separate remapping layer, an exercise generator would either expose technical annotation labels that students are not expected to learn or hide untested grammatical assumptions inside application code. Formal remapping makes those assumptions explicit, testable, correctable, and reusable.

3. Why remapping requires grammatical interpretation

A head word is not always the complete target

The old woman living in the basement needs help.

The source analysis identifies a structural head, but the useful Subject target is the complete phrase, including its determiner, adjective, and postmodifier.

Similar source structures may have different functions

A prepositional phrase may be an Adverbial, a selected complement, a postmodifier, a predicative unit, or an ambiguous attachment. A shortcut such as “every oblique phrase is A” would create neat-looking but unreliable questions.

A classroom category may combine several pieces of evidence

Paul is a good student.

The copular configuration licenses two pedagogical units: is as P and a good student as SC. No single source label supplies the full analysis.

Some units are discontinuous

Did the children play?

The auxiliary is Operator, while the lexical verb is P. The data model must therefore support more than one target span.

4. Worked examples

Direct correspondence

She meant no harm.

Answer: DO — Direct Object. The complete noun phrase is a continuous and unambiguous object target.

Structurally derived analysis

The milk seems off.

Answer: off is SC. The analysis depends on recognising a linking-verb pattern and the predicative relation to the Subject.

Clause functioning as a sentence element

To have seen him lose his temper was unexpected.

Answer: S — Subject. The whole perfect infinitival clause fills the Subject position in the larger clause.

Expert-review guard

It is important to know the truth.

The construction contains a formal Subject and a postponed infinitival Subject. Treating it as an ordinary lexical Subject would conceal the construction.

5. Quality, uncertainty, and the reviewed core

The 106 reviewed cases are not merely examples. They form an executable contract: they establish terminology, target extent, exceptions, and non-automation policies. Later versions must reproduce them exactly or document an approved correction.

  1. Do not confuse frequency with certainty. Common patterns may still be ambiguous.
  2. Do not generalise from one attractive example. A rule must survive contrasting cases.
  3. Do not use a confidence score as grammatical proof. Numerical confidence is not theoretical justification.
  4. Prefer omission to misinformation. Unresolved cases belong in expert review, not in a student quiz.

The current review set includes difficult prepositional attachments, duration phrases, formal and postponed Subjects, object complements, reduced clauses, zero relatives, restrictive and non-restrictive relatives, comparative clauses, purpose–result ambiguity, and clauses whose higher function depends on the matrix predicate.

6. From English to a multilingual resource

The longer-term aim is not to impose the English profile on every language. The reusable architecture consists of corpus ingestion, a machine-readable source analysis, a formal rule schema, target spans, review workflow, question formats, and the practice interface. Each new language still requires its own approved teaching vocabulary, grammar profile, reviewed reference cases, explanations, and expert validation.

Transfer principle

The platform is shared; the grammar is language-specific.

### The current generated bank is not yet a formal application of Damjan and Martin's remapping

The Stanza annotation stage is valid and should be retained. The problem is downstream:

- `scripts/extract_tagset.py` extracts only the permitted answer labels, prompts, modes, and subskills.
- `scripts/build_gold_index.py` preserves the 106 reviewed questions and their hashes.
- `scripts/generate_questions.py` independently assigns pedagogical outputs through a small set of hard-coded `if/elif` conditions over UPOS, XPOS, lemmas, and dependency relations.
- The 106 reviewed mappings are therefore reference anchors, but they are **not the executable rule system that generates the new corpus questions**.

Do not describe the current 34,858 automatically generated questions as having been generated by the reviewed remapping. They were generated by a provisional hand-coded extrapolation.

The purpose of this task is to make the claim literally true:

> Every automatically generated Sentence Elements or Clauses question must be traceable to a versioned, executable remapping rule derived from the Martin-reviewed 106-case contract.

Parts of Speech must use a separate, explicit, versioned word-class mapping profile because the 106 reviewed cases do not constitute a complete POS gold set.

## 4. Formal rule model

Use the supplied `schema/formal_remap_rule.schema.json` as a starting contract. Refine it if necessary, but retain these concepts.

Every rule must contain:

- stable `rule_id`;
- semantic version;
- language/profile;
- layer and output dimension;
- priority;
- `direct`, `rule-based`, or `manual-review` decision class;
- one or more source case IDs from Martin's contract;
- declarative Stanza/UD graph conditions;
- explicit exclusions and guards;
- target-span strategy;
- pedagogical output label;
- publication action: `publish`, `review`, or `reject`;
- teacher note provenance;
- explanation template or explanation policy.

The matcher must be able to inspect at least:

- token form and lemma;
- UPOS;
- XPOS;
- morphological features;
- dependency relation and subtype;
- head properties;
- child and descendant relations;
- auxiliaries and copulas;
- active/passive voice;
- clause markers;
- coordination;
- punctuation;
- relevant lexical classes such as linking verbs and content nouns;
- clause finiteness and verb form;
- dependency subtree boundaries;
- zero-marker situations inferred only when a safe structural rule exists;
- competing rule matches.

Target extraction must support:

- a single token;
- a full dependency subtree;
- a finite or non-finite clause subtree;
- a predicate complex;
- coordinated units;
- discontinuous targets;
- multiple pedagogical units in one example.

Do not use arbitrary floating-point “confidence” as a substitute for linguistic logic. A candidate is publishable because a reviewed rule matches and all guards pass—not because a developer assigned `0.96`.

A numeric confidence may be retained for ranking within review queues, but it must not determine the linguistic truth status.

## 6. Linguistic requirements that must survive formalisation

The contract itself remains authoritative. The following list highlights recurring requirements and known traps; it does not replace the contract.

### 6.1 Sentence elements

Implement or guard at least:

- `nsubj` and full subject-phrase span → `S`;
- clausal subjects, with precise structure where available;
- expletive/formal `it` plus postponed clausal subject as a construction, not plain lexical `S`;
- lexical predicator;
- split predicator:
  - auxiliary in the operator position → `Operator`;
  - lexical nucleus → `P`;
- passive predicate complexes;
- copular predicates;
- linking verbs distinct from the copula;
- verb-particle constructions;
- noun-phrase, pronoun, finite-clause, zero-that, and wh-clause direct objects;
- control/complement constructions routed according to the reviewed policy;
- indirect objects, including reviewed clausal/fused-relative recipient cases;
- subject complements realised by adjective phrase, noun phrase, PP, infinitival clause, -ing clause, and finite clause;
- object complements realised by nominal, adjectival/resultative, PP, infinitival, -ing, and wh-clause structures;
- adverbials realised by adverb, PP, temporal/duration phrase, direction/location, infinitival purpose clause, finite adverbial clause, and reduced/non-finite clause;
- ambiguity guards for PP attachment and copular-versus-adverbial PPs;
- multiple adverbials when the reviewed example requires more than one target.

Never implement a general `obl → A` rule without structural and ambiguity guards.

### 6.2 Clauses

Keep separate dimensions where the teaching framework separates them:

- clause type;
- marker type;
- clause structure;
- clause function.

Implement or guard at least:

- main clauses;
- nominal content clauses:
  - finite that;
  - zero-that;
  - coordinated complements;
  - finite wh;
  - whether/if;
  - nominal relatives;
  - dependent verbal questions;
  - copular subject-complement clauses;
- content clauses after nouns:
  - distinguish appositive clauses from ordinary postmodification where Martin requires it;
- clauses complementing adjectives:
  - preserve the reviewed PostM analysis where specified;
- adverbial clauses:
  - temporal;
  - causal;
  - conditional;
  - negative conditional;
  - concessive;
  - purpose;
  - place/free-choice;
  - manner/comparison;
  - supplementive non-finite;
  - preposition + -ing;
  - reduced/verbless;
- do not merge purpose and result/consequence;
- comparative clauses modifying an adjective must not automatically be labelled `A`;
- relative clauses:
  - relative pronoun;
  - prepositional relative pronoun;
  - relative adverb;
  - zero relative;
  - restrictive;
  - non-restrictive;
  - postmodifier function;
  - punctuation-sensitive and punctuation-unreliable guards;
- marker distinctions:
  - complementizer;
  - interrogative subordinator;
  - subordinating conjunction;
  - relative pronoun;
  - relative adverb;
  - infinitival marker;
  - zero marker;
- structure distinctions:
  - finite that-clause;
  - finite zero-that clause;
  - finite wh-clause;
  - finite whether/if-clause;
  - to-infinitival clause;
  - perfect infinitival structure where Martin's comment requires precision;
  - bare infinitival clause;
  - gerundial versus participial -ing where the available evidence safely supports it;
  - preposition + -ing clause;
  - reduced/verbless clause;
  - comparative clause;
- functions:
  - S;
  - DO;
  - SC;
  - A;
  - PostM;
  - OC/clausal object only where the reviewed policy permits it.

Where the workbook says `Needs manual review`, build a guard. Do not “solve” it by widening an automatic rule.

### 6.3 Teacher comments

Martin's comments must be represented in one of three ways:

1. implemented in rule output or terminology;
2. implemented as an exclusion/manual guard;
3. recorded as unresolved and listed for Damjan/Martin.

Examples include:

- `Operator` in split predicators;
- perfect infinitive precision;
- gerundial versus participial -ing;
- linking verb versus copula;
- appositive clause terminology;
- clause as PostM of an adjective;
- purpose versus result;
- comparative PostM rather than A;
- supplementive clause status;
- restrictive/non-restrictive interpretation when commas are absent;
- ambiguous PP and possible SC analysis.

Do not erase Slovene comments after implementation. Preserve them as internal provenance, excluded from the public payload.

- coordinator;
